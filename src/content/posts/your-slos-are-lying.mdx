Every SLO dashboard I have ever inherited was green. Every team that owned one had also been paged the week before. Both facts were true at the same time, which tells you something about the dashboard.

## The three lies

**Lie one: availability measured at the load balancer.** The load balancer returned 200. The page rendered an error banner. Your users experienced an outage. Your SLO experienced nothing.

**Lie two: latency averaged.** A p50 of 80ms hides a p99 of 4 seconds. The 1 percent of users hitting 4 seconds are the ones with the most data, which means the ones paying you the most.

**Lie three: the SLI that nobody argued about.** If product, engineering and support all agreed on the SLI in one meeting, it is because nobody had to give anything up. Real SLIs cost someone something.

## What to do instead

Start at the user. Write down the three things a user does that, if broken, would make them leave. Measure those from as close to the user as you can get. Synthetic checks from outside your network. Real user monitoring in the client. Business events in the database.

Then pick a percentile that hurts. If p99 latency does not make the engineering lead flinch, it is not the right number.

Finally, set the target lower than you think. An SLO of 99.9 percent that you actually enforce beats 99.99 percent that everyone quietly ignores.
